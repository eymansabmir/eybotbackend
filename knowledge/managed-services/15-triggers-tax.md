# Customer Triggers — Tax and Finance Operate (TFO)

## “We lack visibility on how tax changes will hit our bottom line.”

- **What it may indicate:** Weak forward-looking tax insight, fragmented data and limited scenario visibility.
- **EY MS conversation move:** Position TFO reporting, analytics and dashboards with compliance monitoring and management MIS so tax impacts can be tracked and communicated earlier.
- **Value to position:** Better visibility into obligations, exposures and financial-statement impacts; stronger stakeholder communication.
- **Discovery question:** Which tax changes currently create the most uncertainty for cash flow, provisions or board reporting?

## “Our tax team spends most of its time on coordination, data wrangling and rework.”

- **What it may indicate:** Spine activities (data, coordination, repository, internal reporting) are consuming specialist capacity.
- **EY MS conversation move:** Offer operated tax-function support covering data management, repository, internal coordination, reporting and audit support under defined governance.
- **Value to position:** Free tax leaders for strategic work; reduce rework and people dependency.
- **Discovery question:** What share of tax FTE time goes to data gathering and coordination versus technical judgement and planning?

## “We cannot attract or retain the right tax talent — and a few people hold all the knowledge.”

- **What it may indicate:** Skills gap plus key-person / business-continuity risk.
- **EY MS conversation move:** Position co-sourced or managed access to tax SMEs with standardized processes, documentation and knowledge transfer so delivery is people-independent.
- **Value to position:** Continuity, scarce-skill access and reduced individual dependency.
- **Discovery question:** Which tax areas create the sharpest SME gaps, and what happens if key individuals are unavailable at filing or audit time?

## “We are not confident in our tax data or our ability to respond to information requests on time.”

- **What it may indicate:** Data quality, repository and compliance-visibility gaps.
- **EY MS conversation move:** Shape a tax data architecture / repository and compliance-management operate construct with validation, workflows and response discipline.
- **Value to position:** Higher data reliability, faster accurate responses and lower compliance risk.
- **Discovery question:** Where do data breaks and late responses most often occur — systems, process ownership or documentation?

## “Tax laws keep changing and we struggle to keep pace across all tax areas.”

- **What it may indicate:** Need for specialized coverage and change-management capacity beyond the current bench.
- **EY MS conversation move:** Position modular TFO coverage (compliance, advisory support, controversy, change management) with people–process–technology levers and escalation to SMEs.
- **Value to position:** Broader specialty coverage and adaptable operating model as regulations change.
- **Discovery question:** Which tax domains (direct, indirect, TP, customs, exchange control) create the most stretch today?

## “Is our tax function adding value as a strategic business partner — or only filing returns?”

- **What it may indicate:** Over-weight on routine compliance; under-investment in planning, risk and business partnering.
- **EY MS conversation move:** Separate run compliance into a managed construct so retained leadership can focus on planning, risk, controversy strategy and business partnering.
- **Value to position:** Strategic focus, earlier risk/opportunity alerts and stronger tax–business collaboration.
- **Discovery question:** Which strategic tax activities are delayed because routine compliance consumes capacity?

## “We need to do more with less in tax.”

- **What it may indicate:** Cost and productivity pressure on the tax function.
- **EY MS conversation move:** Baseline tax throughput (tax costs plus tax function costs), process variation and automation potential; shape a scalable full/partial/co-sourced operate model.
- **Value to position:** Efficiency, scalability and lower investment versus building all capability in-house.
- **Discovery question:** Is the priority cost reduction, quality/risk improvement, or releasing capacity for strategy — or all three?

## “We are exploring outsourcing or co-sourcing the tax function.”

- **What it may indicate:** Direct opening for TFO operating-model design.
- **EY MS conversation move:** Discuss modular options — full tax function operation, partial towers (direct/indirect/reporting/data/compliance), or process-based operate — including transition and re-insourcing paths.
- **Value to position:** Flexible model with governance, SPOC, escalation and continuity.
- **Discovery question:** Which activities must stay retained for control or strategy, and which are candidates to operate externally first?

## “Compliance quality is inconsistent — we worry about penalties, filings and exposures.”

- **What it may indicate:** Process, governance and risk-management weakness.
- **EY MS conversation move:** Position end-to-end risk-managed compliance with monitoring, formal sign-off, governance cadence and exposure identification/mitigation.
- **Value to position:** Improved compliance quality, timely filing, reduced penalties and clearer risk posture.
- **Discovery question:** Which compliance streams have the highest residual risk, late filings or rework today?

## “We want tax technology and automation but cannot justify building it alone.”

- **What it may indicate:** Technology investment barrier; opportunity for tech-enabled managed tax operations.
- **EY MS conversation move:** Position TFO technology enablement — data framework, automated validation, workflows, helpdesk, secure transfer and executive dashboards — as part of the operate construct rather than a standalone build.
- **Value to position:** Faster tech enablement with lower internal build burden.
- **Discovery question:** Which tax processes are most manual today, and what reporting or control outcomes would justify automation first?
