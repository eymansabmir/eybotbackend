# Tax and Finance Operate (TFO)

Source grounding: Tax & Finance Operate (TFO) playbook — Tax Operate managed services, operating model, transformation levers and benefits.

## What Tax and Finance Operate is

Tax and Finance Operate (TFO) is EY’s managed tax operating model. The tax function can be fully outsourced, partially outsourced, co-sourced, or operated on a specific-process basis.

The model combines:

- Tax professionals and subject matter experts.
- Standardized, risk-managed processes.
- Technology-enabled operations (data architecture, automation, workflows, dashboards).

Objective: improve efficiency, governance, compliance visibility and strategic decision-making — so tax leadership can focus on value delivery rather than coordination and rework.

## Why organizations need a modern tax operating model

CEOs and CFOs typically ask:

- How do we manage tax and reputation risks and proactively identify tax planning opportunities?
- Have opportunities to transform the tax function been explored and implemented?
- How do we get forward-looking tax insights for cash-flow planning, stakeholder communications and financial-statement impact prediction?
- How will tax changes impact the bottom line — and where is visibility lacking?
- Is the tax function an agile, strategic business partner?
- How does the tax function do more with less?

Tax Heads typically ask:

- How do we ensure industry best practices in the tax function?
- How do we access subject matter experts across all areas of taxation?
- Are we prepared to keep pace with changes in the tax landscape?
- How do we attract and retain tax talent while reducing people dependency?
- How do we reduce time on internal coordination and data management and focus on value delivery?
- How confident are we in responding to regulatory information requests accurately and on time?

## Industry findings that support TFO conversations

Common survey themes used in GTM conversations:

- Many organizations are not highly confident in tax data management capabilities.
- Tax technical skills of personnel often require further augmentation.
- Companies are exploring changes to current tax operating models.
- Co-sourcing is seen as a way to access tax SMEs, increase operational efficiency and improve scalability of the tax function.

## Challenges faced by tax teams

Current challenges:

- Inadequate processes and limited visibility over tax compliances.
- Poor data quality and ineffective risk management.
- Significant time spent on coordination and rework.
- Difficulty attracting and retaining talent; heavy dependency on specific individuals.
- Constantly changing tax laws and regulations requiring specialized expertise.
- Inability to accurately quantify tax throughput (tax costs plus tax function costs).

External drivers:

- Increasing government digitization initiatives.
- Growing tax transparency requirements.
- Increasing business agility requirements.
- Frequent legislative and regulatory changes.

Expectations from tax teams:

- Access to expert advice and industry best practices.
- Complete visibility into tax compliances.
- People-independent processes.
- Identification and mitigation of tax exposures and risks.
- Streamlined data management and repositories.
- Intelligent data and reporting for decision-making.
- More management time for strategic business support.

## Flexible operating model

The model is modular and can be tailored:

### Full tax function operation

End-to-end management of the tax function.

### Partial tax function operation

Support for specific areas such as:

- Direct taxes.
- Indirect taxes.
- Internal tax reporting.
- Compliance management.
- Tax data management.

Engagement constructs may be fully outsourced, partially outsourced, co-sourced, or operated on a specific-process basis.

## Business continuity and transition

Transition to managed services typically covers:

- Process transfer.
- Knowledge transfer.
- Data transfer.
- Operational handover.

Re-insourcing is supported through structured transition plans, documentation/repository management and knowledge-transfer mechanisms — so activities can move back in-house if required.

## Key benefits of a managed tax function

### Quality and governance

- Access to tax experts and industry-leading practices.
- Improved compliance quality; timely filing and reduced penalties.
- Reduced risk of non-compliance.
- Formal sign-off and governance processes.
- Better visibility into compliance status, obligations, litigation matters and performance outcomes.

### Flexibility and efficiency

- Scalable operating model with dedicated SPOC and escalation framework.
- Adaptability to changing tax regulations.
- Reduced dependency on individual employees.
- More organized tax data management and faster response to business requirements.
- Ability to transition activities in-house if required.

### Strategic focus

- Management focus on core business objectives and strategic priorities.
- Stronger tax–business partnership and decision-making support.
- Earlier identification of issues, opportunities and risks.
- Reduced investment burden versus building tax talent and technology fully in-house.

### Tax technology enablement

- Tax data framework and architecture with automated data validation.
- Repository and workflow management tools.
- Virtual tax helpdesk capabilities.
- Secure file transfer and systematic documentation.

## Tax function assessment questions (discovery)

Use these to open a structured TFO diagnostic:

**Strategy and vision**

- What should be the vision and mission of the tax function?
- Is there a more effective way for the tax function to operate?

**Stakeholder expectations**

- What are the expectations of key stakeholders?
- Is the tax function effectively meeting those expectations?

**Tax governance**

- Is sufficient time invested in tax governance and business planning?
- Is the tax function contributing strategically beyond compliance?

**Risk management**

- Can the tax function identify risks proactively?
- Are mitigation actions implemented in a timely manner?

**Talent utilization**

- Are tax professionals spending excessive time on routine compliances?
- Are specialized skills being fully utilized?

**Tax technology**

- Is there adequate focus on tax technology and automation?
- Are technology investments helping reduce risk and improve efficiency?

**Operating model design**

- Should the tax function be partially or fully outsourced / co-sourced?
- What factors should drive the operating model decision?

## When to route to Tax and Finance / TFO

- Tax operating model, co-sourcing, outsourcing or managed tax function → Tax and Finance Operate (TFO).
- Direct/indirect/GST/ITR/customs/transfer pricing/exchange control compliance operate work → TFO Compliance.
- Tax data, repository, MIS, audit support, governance spine → TFO Tax Function Support.
- Tax controversy, APA, PE, planning support in an operate construct → TFO Advisory / Planning / Controversy.
- Digital tax tooling, automation, dashboards as part of tax operate → TFO Technology Enablement (with Digital Tax specialist handoff where needed).
- Pure one-off tax advisory without run/operate scope → qualify carefully; may be advisory rather than Managed Services unless transitioned into a recurring operate model.
